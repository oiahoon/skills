# Character and environment: distinct mark systems in one picture

Read this when a result has a plausible cartoon person but a generic background, or when environmental texture invades skin/clothes. These are visible constructions checked in the full originals, not claims about the artist's tools. They supplement the accepted moderate character-line baseline; they do not require globally thin outlines or an additional texture pass.

## Assign marks by region before generation

| Region | What establishes the form | Interior marks and boundary | Full-original evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Face and hands | A few deliberately shaped near-black contour/feature lines; connected skin planes; bounded shadow shapes | Brows, eyelids, mouth and fingers express this person's action. Hair/stubble, freckles or age marks are local accents. Avoid turning cheek/forehead shading into many tiny rendering strokes. | REF-0038, REF-0027; REF-0053's broad bare torso and hands |
| Hair and garments | Grouped silhouette, broad color masses, selective folds and overlap lines | Marks can follow hair growth, beard edge or a meaningful garment fold/pattern. Keep the underlying color plane readable. Do not import leaf/water dashes onto every garment or thin all contours into portrait detail. | REF-0027, REF-0038, MMM-03-07 |
| Focal birds | Species-specific head/beak/neck/body/joint silhouette; connected feather color planes and grouped wing fans | Broad feather panels and a few local curved accents describe the wing. Simplify farther birds. Do not spread the habitat's dense marks through the body or draw every feather shaft. This is observed for crows; new species require anatomical inference. | MMM-05-01; REF-0003's distant bird silhouettes are not close-animal calibration |
| Foliage and earth | Large connected dark/light masses, branch/landform structure, clusters of color strokes | Leaves and bark change stroke length/direction around branches and trunk curvature. Ground marks recede and turn through the path. Gaps and shared base colors connect them into masses. | MMM-01-07: tiny simply drawn person within a much denser canopy/path |
| Water and reflections | Base water color plus clustered horizontal dashes, arcs and broken reflected-color groups | Flow, hulls, distance and local light redirect/group marks. Leave some water calm. Reflections are color fragments, not photorealistic shiny puddles. | REF-0053: quiet skin/shirt/trousers beside dense marked water and boat interiors |
| Walls, furniture and wood | Selective structural contours, unequal broad planes and readable perspective | Wood uses longer grain strokes aligned with the surface and perspective; masonry can use sparse patches or repeated units. Background structure is not a requirement to outline every texture stroke. | MMM-03-07's quiet clothes/walls versus long floor and bed marks; REF-0043's pale wall |
| Weather, sky and light | A chosen broad sky/cloud shape or weather direction, with localized mark groups | Rain may cross subject and background as a weather layer. It must not become the skin/cloth texture. Sky and water need different flows even if both use rounded marks. Choose light treatment from sky-and-light.md. | REF-0052; REF-0038's comparatively quiet night sky |

These are regional decisions, not a foreground/background paint filter. A foreground plant still uses plant marks; a distant person still uses simplified cartoon shape. Quiet characters beside active scenery are common, but an interior may keep both relatively quiet. Do not impose a numerical texture quota.

## Connect the systems without flattening their difference

Use one viewpoint, contact/occlusion, local color relationships and a coherent shadow direction to join subject and setting. Let a foliage shadow on a shirt remain a few bounded color shapes; let reflected light on a face remain a limited color plane. The connection does not require copying the background's mark field into the person. Preserve clear silhouettes without a cut-out halo; a subject's shadow/contact and nearby overlaps should place it inside the scene.

Select character and environment reference roles explicitly. A whole original remains the image reference: a crop can help the assistant inspect a region but does not replace the full scene's hierarchy. A character reference's detailed painting/screen is not a skin-rendering target. An environment reference's tiny face is not adequate calibration for a close portrait.

## Silhouette volume and surface-specific reference diagnosis

The useful relationships differ by individual. Describing a broad cheek is insufficient if the output retains a large polished hair fan and pointed lower face. A flatter highlight palette alone does not fix the silhouette. Stronger abstraction needs an individual distortion decision too: emphasize selected cheek/head, nose/mouth or posture relationships while preserving identity and expression, rather than applying one cute face or a fixed large-head formula. ## Compact prompt partition

After the scene and reference roles, describe only the regions needed:

> People: [individual silhouette/gesture], naturally drawn moderate near-black contours, sparse expressive face/hand marks, connected skin and garment color planes with bounded shadows; [specific local hair/fabric detail if needed]. Environment: [major masses and structures], [material-specific stroke families and their direction/grouping], [calm regions]. Integration: shared viewpoint, readable contact/overlap and limited shared lighting. Transfer those drawing operations, with the reference content excluded.

Do not append every row of this document to the prompt. In a dry room, choose long wood marks and quiet wall planes rather than inserting water/sky rules. For a landscape, specify how the canopy or water is built before adding small details. Avoid vague requests to make the entire image equally painterly or textured.

## Separate acceptance checks

1. **Character:** at face/hand scale, do contours and broad planes carry the expression/action before texture? Is likeness carried by individual shapes rather than photographic eyelids, skin shading and tiny hair strands? Are local line-weight excesses corrected without making every line thin?
2. **Environment:** at thumbnail scale, do marks combine into canopy, flow, wall or ground masses? At detail scale, are their direction, size and density specific to each surface rather than a repeated global stamp? Does the room remain drawn rather than a naturalistic scene behind a cartoon?
3. **Boundary and integration:** do contact/occlusion and light place the person in the setting while skin/clothes keep their own construction? Has an environmental edit changed identity/gesture or injected a repeated texture into the person?

A pass in one region cannot compensate for a failure in the other. Record which region improved. A reference-grounded rule is not generation reliability; the prior drone/music-shop tests still constrain that claim.
