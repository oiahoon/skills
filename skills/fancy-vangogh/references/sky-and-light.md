## Transferable decisions

**Sky:** Decide whether it supports the character quietly or becomes an active compositional mass. Establish broad color areas and a few dominant movements before describing individual strokes. Do not automatically fill an empty sky, and do not automatically make every sky blank. The originals support both extremes when the composition calls for them.

**Clouds:** Name the cloud structure: open broken bands, a connected bank, or a few large lobes. Use unequal widths, overlaps, light/dark grouping and gaps to make that structure legible at thumbnail scale. Short rounded dashes are valid; the failure is unrelated marks scattered at similar size and spacing without forming a cloud. Horizontal bands are also valid when they form coherent layers. Curves should describe a chosen lobe or flow, not force every cloud into a spiral. Cloud edges can be built by color contrast; do not require black outlines around every cloud or dash.

**Sun and moon:** Decide whether a visible disc is needed at all, then choose its phase, placement, scale and contrast as part of the new composition. A clean flat disc is supported by the originals. A luminous disc can have a few nested color bands or curved strokes; a moon may be partly hidden by clouds. Craters, airbrushed bloom and radial rays are not universal requirements. A generic moon in a night scene is not by itself evidence of copying; borrowing an identifiable arrangement or a source-specific fantasy prop is a different issue.

**Light and water:** Relate a visible light source to nearby cloud color and, when compositionally appropriate, a broken reflection below it. This is stylized coherence, not physically exact ray tracing. Sky arcs may bend around a light or cloud while water strokes remain flatter and more horizontal. Never apply one evenly repeated stamp field across both regions merely to make the image look consistent.

**Surface:** Rounded, smooth-edged and occasionally translucent overlapping marks occur in the originals. Preserve their shape organization; do not translate them into photographic cloud rendering or simulated bristle/canvas texture. Dense spirals are supported in dramatic or quotation-like examples, but are not a mandatory signature for every new picture.

## Prompt and inspect

Use one role-matched sky original when needed, alongside the character reference. State which cloud/light construction may transfer and which depicted content must not. Do not attach all sky modes at once. Inspect the environment anchor's sky before attachment too. A plant reference with an active sky can transmit that mark field even when the prompt requests a quiet opening. Prefer compatible modes or add a specifically assigned quiet-sky original when that operation is missing; this is conditional, not a fixed extra reference/pass.

Separate mark coverage from visual activity. MMM-03-03 covers the dark sky as well as the bright cloud bank with strokes: darker, subtler groups keep the surrounding field subordinate. A sky may therefore be fully marked, including its nearly single-color regions. Plan coherent low-contrast flow through those regions, with stronger light/dark and width changes reserved for the cloud and local halo. Do not manufacture blank gaps as the default repair. When the selected mode actually uses open color planes, locate their connected extent and boundaries; a bounded quiet-plane repair is an option for that mode only. Neither full coverage nor empty openings are mandatory for every sky. For cloud occlusion, inspect the visible circular arc and the cloud edge hiding it: a partially hidden full disc is a different construction from a free-floating crescent.

For the user's preferred IMG_3681 nighttime bench image, a candidate repair direction is: retain the approved cartoon woman, bench, bag, pose and night palette; organize the upper sky into a few connected unequal blue-violet cloud masses with curved internal strokes and broad dark gaps; keep the moon a deliberately placed simple light shape; distinguish the sky's flow from the water's horizontal marks. This is a proposed local repair, not a tested result or a global preset.

Inspect at full-image and thumbnail scale: do the clouds remain shapes after the individual dashes disappear? Are active and calm areas intentional? Does a light source belong to the new scene? Have sky and water kept distinct movement? Has a sky edit changed the approved character or framing? Record those changes rather than calling an edit pixel-exact.
