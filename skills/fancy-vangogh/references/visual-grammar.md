# Transfer grammar for new scenes and unfamiliar objects

This is an operational reading of the registered originals, not a claim about the artist's method. The 95-image catalog was screened as whole-picture contact sheets; the observations below were checked against full-size examples cited by ID. The existing visual-role index remains a selective, evidence-based index rather than a tag for every image. Never imply an uninspected original proves a detailed rule. The routes below are hypotheses for new objects and still need independent image-generation tests.

## Separate what is drawn from how it is drawn

1. **Content and contact come from the user.** Record the person's identity cues, action, object count, held/touched surfaces, camera view and any required setting. Keep an object's recognizable purpose and silhouette even if no original depicts it. REF-0038 shows that a foreground phone and embracing hands are readable through their contact geometry; REF-0012 shows a modern phone, tablet and crowd rendered in the same scene without turning them into period props.
2. **Build large shapes before mark fields.** Decide dominant masses, overlaps, near/far scale, calm/active regions and a focal contrast. In REF-0043, a pale wall occupies much more area than its sparse marks; the doorway and small figure remain legible. REF-0052 holds a dark rainy street with a few warm light anchors. Neither implies a fixed empty-space quota.
3. **Cartoonize the subject structurally.** Keep individualized head/hair silhouette, brow/eye/nose relationships, gesture and bounded shadow planes; redraw with selective near-black contours. REF-0038 uses differently designed adjacent faces and simplified hands. REF-0027 pushes noses, brows and torso proportions further. The literal beard, hat and flower crown are content, not style rules. At crowd scale REF-0012 reduces faces to very few marks rather than drawing every eye as a portrait.
4. **Make surface marks describe a form.** For water, REF-0042 groups short horizontal and arcing color strokes around a figure and reflection; for rain, REF-0052 uses slanted slashes and broken warm pavement chains; for masonry, REF-0043 leaves broad connected wall color under sparse irregular dashes and keeps structural edges distinct. A mark field changes direction, length, spacing and density with material, perspective, light and distance. Do not copy one mark shape across skin, clothing, wall, foliage and sky.
5. **Retain an information hierarchy.** The focal face, hand or contact receives enough distinct lines to read; background crowds, distant structures and secondary objects lose detail. REF-0012 is a useful counterexample to treating every visible face equally. Dense illustrations also exist (REF-0045), but density follows its crowd/architecture composition and is not a global finish pass.

## Extrapolate an object absent from the corpus

Use this sequence for a laptop, helmet, stroller, ceramic wheel, vehicle, modern sign or any unseen object:

- Identify its **function and contacts** first: what supports it, what touches it, what must remain open/closed, which parts the viewer must recognize. Do not substitute a reference prop.
- Reduce it to **one dominant silhouette plus a few structural planes**. Use selective ink contours at important outer edges, hinges, openings and overlaps. Keep straight manufactured geometry readable; the hand-drawn quality comes from small natural variation and graphic color, not bending every line.
- Choose a **base color family, one bounded shadow family and limited accents**. If the material is smooth, a calm connected plane is allowed. Use a few aligned marks only where they explain light, wear or curvature; do not force foliage/water dashes onto plastic or glass. REF-0012's devices and REF-0043's wall show that low-mark planes can coexist with active surroundings.
- Integrate it through **occlusion, contact shadow and shared viewpoint**. In fisheye scenes, apply the same projection to the unfamiliar object and nearby architecture/ground rather than enlarging just one hand.
- For mixed surfaces, choose the distinguishing edge/plane operation for each material separately. Check glass visibility, metal highlights and ceramic opacity beside skin/cloth and wood; requesting a convincing object must not silently switch the whole drawing into material rendering. These modern finish choices are inference, not original evidence for every object.
- Put the active pen strokes on a **scene-appropriate neighbor** if the object itself is quiet: weather, plants, water, wood, ground or sky. The drawing must remain recognizably in the same language as the character.

This is a synthesis from varied originals, not a license to imitate a particular painting, duplicate a copyrighted composition or assert how the artist would definitively draw an unseen object. If the new object dominates the picture, inspect REF-0012 for modern flat devices, REF-0021 for an unusual large contact silhouette, or another full original with comparable geometry. Use the reference for its operation only.

## Containers, transparent boundaries and submerged contents

For an aquarium, jar or vase, establish what is behind the glass, what is outside it, the rim/frame thickness and the supporting/contact planes. Quiet contained water can retain connected color rather than borrow open-sea waves or reflection chains. Infer this from object function and the originals’ plane/edge language, not a claim that a sea original demonstrates aquariums. Inspect the visible contents, pointing/holding hand and count on their actual sides of the boundary. Glass/frame reflections should not accidentally replace requested visible eyes in clear eyewear. A wheelchair/aquarium revision retained device supports and three fish while simplifying scale detail into larger fish planes; eye visibility recovered, but plant groups and original-style fish expression remained partial. This supports a conditional content/material decision, not a proven fish or mobility-device recipe.

## Visual facts when an object has no reliable name

Keep an observed shape separate from an inferred object class. Record what distinguishes it: outer contour and endpoints, concave/convex sides, open versus closed negative space, orientation, size relative to hand/body/support, and the visible contact points. Preserve diagnostic garment/headwear geometry too, rather than replacing a folded cap with a familiar tied bandana. Describe only needed visual facts, not hidden anatomy or an invented manufacturing function.

For example, an unfamiliar curved clay piece can be described as a solid bent slab with a convex outer edge, an open left concavity and upper/lower hand contacts. “Pottery object” alone allowed a semantic diagnostic to substitute a vase. The more explicit geometry prevented that class substitution in another sample, but scale, shirt/cap construction and likeness still drifted. Therefore compare the output to the source; the shape description is not a guaranteed reconstruction or a reason to remove the photograph from normal photo translation.

## Reference routing and contamination checks

Choose a primary full original for the overall drawing organization, with a character/interaction or environment original added for an operation it lacks and at most one conditional sky/weather/light example. A suitable drawn character guide can already supply the character operation; it does not require a redundant face original in every scene pass. Pick by the operation that is hardest in this request. A city perspective can use REF-0015 for steep convergence, but its billboards, logos and painter must stay behind; the calmer REF-0043 is safer when only a street wall and structure are needed. A rain scene can use REF-0052 for weather and light without copying its bicyclist, lamps or carried picture. A modern crowd can use REF-0012 for varied figure detail without copying the bus, tablet or exact people. Famous starry skies, sunflowers, moon props and the recurring painter are high-contamination content; use those originals only when their specific drawing operation cannot be shown by a less motif-heavy example.

Before generation, state privately or in the saved prompt: **source facts to keep; reference operation to transfer; depicted content to exclude; unknown object inference; expected calm and active regions**. After generation, check the silhouette/contact, cartoon face, material-specific marks, large-mass hierarchy and copied motifs separately. A visually pleasing result is not proof that these rules generalize; validate a revision on a different scene before promoting it as reliable.

## First probe, not a settled rule

A 2026-09-30 unseeded tram-stop/drone probe compared three role references with two. Both kept the unfamiliar drone and hand/tool contact recognizable. The three-reference result drifted toward polished animation faces and glossy pavement; a stronger cartoon-face original plus the rain original yielded more graphic faces while preserving the drone, though its wet ground remained over-detailed. The prompts changed along with references, so this does **not** isolate a causal effect or prove that two references are always better. It supports keeping the character source visually strong and omitting an object reference when the object's geometry can be stated clearly. See the internal development record in the project archive. A subsequent dry music-shop scene kept a synthesizer and plug contact readable but reverted to generic animation-style people and interior; the style-transfer claim remains unvalidated. A next test should change conditioning strategy, not merely add synonyms to the same prompt.

For the distinct construction of faces, garments, foliage, water and built objects within one picture, use [character/environment mark separation](character-environment.md). The distinction follows surface and visual role, not a blanket foreground/background texture filter.
