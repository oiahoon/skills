# Executable routes

Use the host's available image generator. When style images are unavailable, follow the text-only style guidance in [the skill entry](../SKILL.md#reference-inputs). These recipes describe roles and decisions, not invented API weights or a fixed stage count. Select full originals by [drawing rules](drawing-rules.md); use [calibration](evidence.md) to distinguish accepted direction from experimental options.

## Text → complete illustration

Extract entities, interaction, mood, framing and required visible contacts. Select a primary whole-drawing original plus other originals only for missing operations. Name the actual region to transfer, especially when an original contains a painted portrait. Generate one complete scene with the compact core in SKILL.md, then inspect subject drawing, material organization, contact and framing separately.

A complex close-up around a technical object can return rendered hair/skin/hands even without a raw photo. Correct device geometry does not qualify the subject drawing. After diagnosing that failure, a useful generated contact drawing may supply geometry beside an explicit focal-person original for a structural head/hand redraw; inspect actual feature/plane changes and protected object contacts afterward. This is a conditional revision, not a mandatory guide for text scenes. In the latest workshop probe it improved the head/wood while window foliage and shared fisheye remained unresolved.

Dry snow need not inherit water reflections or foliage dashes. New species/objects require inference from their silhouette, joints, openings and function; use [transfer grammar](visual-grammar.md). A successful content structure does not establish a style pass.

## Photo → direct complete illustration

Attach the original photo as content and selected originals as drawing references. Identify individual head/hair/feature relationships, expression, clothing, meaningful occlusions and the subject-contact group. For background replacement, preserve used supports, held objects, companions and relevant foreground props; name the new setting's major forms and placements positively.

> Redraw the individual and moment in image A in the drawing language of B/C. Keep [identity-bearing shapes, gesture, counts, contact]. Subjects use [specific contour/feature decisions and connected planes]; environment uses [new layout and material-specific groups]. [Framing/viewpoint]. Transfer drawing operations without borrowing reference identities or story props.

Inspect for photographic arrangement/modeling, a generic replacement face, unrequested set dressing and reference motifs. Keep direct translation available, but use the available evidence for this source/task: repeated strands, portrait feature construction or scenery overcontrol are a reason to select the conditional reconstruction route immediately. A new conversation or another backdrop does not erase that known failure. Do not extrapolate one failed source into a rule that every photo needs a guide.

A new background/viewpoint requested from a photo still uses that original as content. A previous full illustration is an edit target when the user asks to revise it. Keep that distinction in saved attachment roles.

## Conditional photo guide → complete scene

Use when direct/photo conditioning overcontrols subject or environment rendering, or when a suitable drawn subject already exists. It is not a required pipeline.

### Establish the drawn subject/contact group

Attach the photo and a role-matched character original. Draw the relevant person and contacts on a neutral background: source-specific silhouette/feature decisions, moderate near-black contours, connected colors and bounded shadows. Keep individual expression, occlusions, important patterns and viewpoint. Include a seat/table/strap/props when they determine contact or counts; omit replaceable scenery. Do not invent eyes beneath tinted lenses, fringe or masks.

If the requested projection differs substantially from the photo, the guide should establish that body/held-object projection too. Preserving the photo's old geometry makes the later scene fight the new view. Compare likeness and drawn construction separately; hidden anatomy is inference.

### Inspect before integration

Compare against the photo, not just the stage label. Check head/hair/body relationships, expression, contacts, object openings/scale, pattern groups and required visible extent. For eyes, explicitly check white/pupil/lid relationships when visible, gaze beneath eyewear and brow asymmetry; a flat face with dot eyes is not automatically a suitable guide. Inspect linked head/body contours and selective line emphasis, not just whether shading was reduced. A neutral-background portrait with flat photo-light fragments is still a failed cartoon guide; use [character construction](character-environment.md). A simpler face that replaces the individual is also insufficient. If an intended subject guide recreates the photo's furniture or repeats one mark family over floor, garment and body, record that missed scope explicitly; it may preserve useful contact content without qualifying as a finished guide. A corrected backdrop alone does not fix the head or animal construction.

When contacts are useful but the head construction fails, diagnose what remains before choosing a bounded redraw:
- Many alternating dark/light swept ribbons can still render hair strands even when each ribbon is wide. Replace that local ribbon system with a connected base and unequal larger structural lobes, preserving the actual part/sweep; do not round every haircut into a cap.
- A clear-lens eye with a full white disk may need an actual skin/lid plane covering the opening/pupil; smaller circles alone do not change its expression. Check the lid boundary inside the lens separately from the frame. Tinted or hidden eyes follow the photo's visibility instead.
- Quiet shading can retain traced nose/lip anatomy, a generic face or heavy uniform contours. Rebuild the source-specific head/feature relationships and linked contour hierarchy, not merely its finish.

For that diagnosed redraw, a useful source-derived drawing can supply identity, body and contacts beside a full ordinary-figure original supplying the failing regional operation; omit the raw photo when it was restoring photographic rendering. Do not import the donor's eye mode or identity. Inspect the changes, source likeness and protected contacts afterward. If the same construction persists, change a meaningful conditioning choice or record the limit; do not stack a fixed repair chain. This is an option, not a mandatory extra generation. A partial guide may be tested diagnostically in a scene only with its failure recorded; it is not thereby a qualified ordinary-use guide. If such an integration keeps the same failed nose/lip/hair or fur construction, stop treating whole-scene integration as its repair. Return to the earlier subject-construction decision or retain the stronger prior drawing with a stated limit; do not make another backdrop the next cure. A later cat/photo test preserved useful contacts but reproduced the failed portrait/fur finish in the courtyard. Normal-use integration still requires a usable drawn subject.

Contour-only studies are experimental diagnostics. They can return colored portraits, heavy silhouettes or many hair ribbons; judge the actual output and do not count an ignored format request as success. Small guide marks need not all become finish highlights or identity requirements.

### Generate the whole scene

Attach the usable guide and suitable full originals; omit the raw photograph to avoid restoring its rendering/arrangement. A guide already supplying good character construction can need only one primary interior/environment original. Add others for missing operations, not a fixed count. Primacy is a stated drawing role, not a numerical tool weight or an attachment-order guarantee.

Preserve the guide's useful feature/contact/plane decisions without refining them into shiny hair, portrait facets or extra cloth rendering. Design a complete setting with shared perspective, overlaps and bounded light. Do not require empty-background/character/integration passes or a paint-overlay stage.

For over-detailed scenery, establish the requested major masses and quiet/active hierarchy first. For incidental reference furniture/pictures, positively assign new openings, supports and relevant object placements. If material construction still fails, reconsider the primary original rather than adding exclusions indefinitely. A simpler courtyard or quiet wall is an option, not a universal cleaner-background rule; rich E/G environmental direction remains the calibration.

Specify final-canvas scale independently of the guide crop: required head/hand placement and visible body extent when needed. If the result still enlarges the group, a coordinated wider-view re-layout can pull the camera back and rescale its supports/near structures together, rather than shrinking people alone. The requested coordinates are a drawing plan, not a placement guarantee; inspect the resulting group extent, support scale and contacts. Do not claim a foot/object contact is correct when cropped or hidden.

Compare the final result against both guide and photo: face/gaze, hands, props/counts, patterns, supports and framing can change. This is a new drawing, not an exact composite or approved likeness merely because a guide was used.

## Bound a revision by content and drawing operations

For a useful complete illustration, state the protected subject construction/contact group, the region to change, the full original's precise operation and the replacement region's large shapes. A plant donor can supply internal bent crown groups without supplying its skyline; a wood donor can supply board-aligned bands without supplying its furniture. Inspect protected regions after generation because these are redraws, not exact composites. If useful contacts come from an otherwise failed drawing, explicitly protect their content geometry while allowing the failed head, edge or shadow construction to be replaced; do not label the whole existing finish as protected.

When a photo-derived scene acquired extra props, build an explicit inventory from the source and positively assign the replacement support/wall/opening. Count meaningful foreground/held objects and inspect complete handles/openings and supports. Do not assume “same props” removes earlier hallucinations. Preserve intentional user-requested scene additions; an inventory is a request-specific content bound, not a ban on creative setting design. A revised camera scene retained liked head drawing while removing extra equipment/decor and restoring a visible teapot handle; wood finish remained only partly improved.

## Optional content and regional-style inspection

When `inspect_fancy_pair` is available or explicitly requested, inspect its current schema before calling; do not assume an older interface. Use `pair` for source-content preservation. Prefer its `style` mode for original/candidate drawing comparisons when supported: specify the candidate and the schema's 2–3 full references with a reference region, candidate region and a small relevant set of dimension IDs. This inspection reference count is not a fixed generation attachment count. Ordinary people and a depicted portrait, foreground flowers and far crowns, or wood and water must not become interchangeable standards. Allow changed identity, layout, literal hues and requested viewpoint; hidden details or unmatched material/scale remain unverified.

Keep per-reference, per-dimension results; do not majority-vote a pass. A verdict can contradict its difference code or crude location. The current service's `needs_visual_review` flags such problems, but false does not mean a person already reviewed the image. `description` and `adjustment` can be rubric templates derived from limited choices, not model-written visual evidence. Manually confirm the candidate, the source region and the specific operation before applying even a noncontradictory proposal. A broad color-plane “similar” result does not certify petal edge treatment or every neighboring region. Preserve unsupported suggestions and disagreements rather than forcing them into rule changes.

For `pair`, use a few named visible predicates, preferably one relation per check; distinguish direct touch from connection through stems/supports. Save exact arguments, input hashes, pair order and raw verdicts. An absence-check may return “not conforming” precisely because a defect is absent. Probabilities and concentration are not calibrated correctness, style scores or human acceptance. The checker is an optional local aid, not an import dependency, required extra generation stage or whole-style certificate. Apply one or two visually confirmed changes while protecting the successful drawing, and test another scene before widening the rule.

## Revision, viewpoint and stopping

Diagnose the failing region and restate protected drawing/content before editing. Inspect neighboring regions afterward; supposedly protected identity, action, materials and framing may change. A changed reference can target a missing hair/contour operation while a useful eye expression comes from the edit target; explicitly separate those roles rather than accepting the donor's entire face template. Preserve accepted qualities when further polishing weakens them.

For fisheye, inspect the person, held objects, architecture/trees and ground together; curved pavement and an enlarged hand are not enough. Check projection separately from drawing language.

Plan the visible extent of a functional object before enlarging its near part. For example, decide whether a bicycle is fully shown, deliberately cropped, or explicitly a wheel/fork assembly; check the frame/stand and axle contact separately. A convincing close wheel and wrench do not substitute for the requested complete bicycle.

For a strongly changed projection, state the camera's height/tilt and approximate optical center, the near-to-far scale change through the subject/contact group, and how at least two surrounding structural families bend together. Treat these as a drawing plan, not a calibrated lens simulation. Before distortion, plan unequal near/middle/far masses and overlap: choose a framing foreground, a subject/contact region and a distinct farther plane. Use scale, value and unequal detail as well as bent lines. A symmetrical tunnel of equally detailed shelves is not an automatic composition. Window landscapes still need connected crown and distance groups. Inspect style independently: a convincing bowed interior can still surround a photographic face. A structural person redraw can preserve useful projection, but inspect the hair/face/hand identities and object contacts again; a large near hand must retain a drawn contact construction. Reprojection or a wider view can expose unseen shoes, limbs or setting: record those as inference and check the requested visible extent, rather than calling them preserved photo facts. A circular dark border is optional, not proof of a coherent fisheye.

Inspect the planned structural families independently: upper beams/canopy, uprights/openings, and floor/counter/support boundaries. In a viewpoint edit, protect the useful character/object construction separately from the old architecture’s projection. Explicitly allow placement, supports and structure to reproject together. A curved outer doorway or threshold is not evidence that internal floor-board or rear-window families changed; inspect their actual lines and distance relationships. A new bird-hut test kept a quiet head/binocular contact and improved floor/window bends in a retry, but strong shared projection and outside plant mark groups remained partial. Do not count that as an all-family or whole-style pass. A common failure is a huge hand and bowed counter inside a mostly straight room. A diagnosed whole-scene retry can assign a few large sweeping upper arcs, opposed bowed near uprights and a floor/support fan around the same subject-centered space, with unequal near/far masses. This is scene-specific geometry, not a bookshop preset. Use the guide for subject/contact construction while allowing its placement and projection to change together. Do not freeze the guide's crop or ordinary limb scaling.

If that retry also fragments all wood/walls into paint chips or imports the landscape donor's scenery, reconsider the attachment roles and primary original. A guide plus one matched interior original can reduce competition when no additional operation is needed; positively design any window's major crown/ground masses. This does not mean removing rich environmental marks generally, and changing attachment count alone is not a proven cause. Inspect floor/window curvature, window drawing, donor motifs and subject likeness separately after the retry.

When a targeted repair repeats the same failure, change a meaningful reference/conditioning/layout decision or deliver the strongest result with its limitation. In research, label reused/tuned scenes as development inputs rather than independent validation.

When repeated edits preserve a generic/younger head, a localized identity-repair probe may reintroduce the original photo for visible head/hair/glasses relationships beside the role-matched cartoon original. This can recover proportions while also restoring portrait strands or lip facets. If that occurs, treat the corrected drawing as content for a bounded structural redraw with the original, omitting the raw photo again, and inspect identity/expression/contacts afterward. This is a diagnosed option, not a required repair loop, fixed extra stages or proof that the photograph can be given a tool weight. Stop or change the conditioning strategy when the same defect persists.

Character-line revisions are local options: relieve excessive contour swells while retaining simplified anatomy, grouped hair/fur and connected colors. Do not globally thin into portrait strands/creases or thicken all edges into wedges. S/T's accepted balance remains; no historical polishing sequence is required.

## Conditional focal-animal guide

Default to one complete text scene. If a focal animal stays naturalistic after a role-matched reference change, a subject/contact study is an option instead of more feather/fur bans.

Attach a comparable full animal original; infer the requested species from source/text, not the original's species. Draw its silhouette, joints, markings and relevant support/held contacts with connected planes and grouped wing/fur masses. Inspect species/action/limbs and actual drawing; a realistic or incorrectly posed guide should not advance.

Generate the full habitat from a usable guide plus role-matched originals. Keep the guide's body/contact construction while habitat materials remain rich where appropriate. Compare species, gaze, limbs, toes, supports and mark groups again. Limited heron evidence does not establish all-animal reliability; shrinking an eye alone can move a dog toward realism.
